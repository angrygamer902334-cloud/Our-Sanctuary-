Chapter 4: Refraction of Light at Plane Surfaces

1. Refraction of Light: The change in direction of the path of light, when it passes from one transparent medium to another transparent medium, is called refraction of light. It is essentially a surface phenomenon.

2. Laws of Refraction (Snell's Law)

  • The incident ray, the refracted ray, and the normal at the point of incidence, all lie in the same plane.
  • The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence i to the sine of the angle of refraction r is constant for the pair of given media.
Refractive Index (μ) = sin(i) / sin(r) = Speed of light in vacuum (c) / Speed of light in medium (v)

3. Factors Affecting Refractive Index

(a) Nature of the medium: Smaller the speed of light in a medium relative to air, higher is the refractive index of that medium (e.g., μglass ≈ 1.5, μwater ≈ 1.33).

(b) Physical condition (Temperature): With an increase in temperature, the speed of light in the medium increases, so the refractive index decreases.

(c) Wavelength of light: Refractive index is maximum for violet light and minimum for red light in a given medium.
